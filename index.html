Process, mapped out

How I think about a video.

01—07

01

What happens after I send you my footage?

I don’t open the editing software and start cutting immediately. First, I understand the video: who it’s for, what it needs to achieve, and what the viewer should take away from it.

From there, I make the key pre-editing decisions: typography, color palette, visual language, pacing, and the overall direction of the video. This gives the edit a clear visual system before I start building it.

02

How do you decide where to use motion design?

Motion design should have a purpose beyond looking good.

I use it to simplify complex information, introduce new sections, guide attention toward important details, and make key ideas easier to follow. The goal is to use motion where it improves clarity, emphasis, or storytelling, not simply to add more movement.

03

How do you make complex ideas easier to understand visually?

I break complex information into step-by-step visual reveals, focusing the viewer on one key takeaway at a time.

I combine clean typography, relatable visual metaphors, and controlled use of color to create a clear visual hierarchy. A single accent color can also be used strategically to direct attention toward the most important information.

04

How do you choose B-roll and other visual assets?

I don’t choose B-roll simply because it matches the words being said.

I look for atmospheric and metaphorical visuals that support the context, emotion, and overall visual direction of the video. The right B-roll should add meaning, create a feeling, or help the viewer understand the idea, not just fill an empty space.

05

How do you keep a video engaging without over-editing it?

I focus on creating visual variety without making the edit feel chaotic.

I start with a clean rough cut, removing unnecessary pauses, then use J-cuts and L-cuts to maintain continuity. From there, I use subtle techniques such as punch-ins, directional cuts, intentional B-roll, and motion graphics to maintain attention and improve clarity.

The goal isn’t to make something move every second. It’s to give the viewer a reason to keep watching.

06

Can you match an existing visual style or brand?

Yes. Before editing, I assess the existing visual language of the brand, including its color palette, typography, composition, graphic style, and motion language.

I then use those decisions as a visual system throughout the edit, so the final video feels like a natural extension of the brand rather than a separate piece of content.

07

What makes your editing different from other editors?

The first thing I value is reliability. I take deadlines seriously and aim to make the editing process predictable and easy for the client.

Creatively, I want the video to feel specific to you, not like a generic edit. I use intentional visual branding and a consistent visual language to make the content recognizable.

I’m also interested in the psychology behind attention, retention, and persuasion. I think about why someone keeps watching, what makes information memorable, and where visual decisions can strengthen the message.

For me, editing isn’t just about making a video look good. It’s about making the message easier to understand, harder to forget, and worth watching.
